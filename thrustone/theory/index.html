ThrustOne Theory Guide

ThrustOne contains 49 runnable, bounded engineering models. This guide explains their governing relations and the conditions attached to them. It is an original description of the implemented models; it does not reproduce textbook material. For exact inputs, outputs, and limits of an individual model, use the in-app capability catalog and current model description.

All calculations use SI internally. Unless a mode explicitly supplies variable thermodynamic properties, a gas is modeled with constant specific heat and ratio of specific heats. Results apply only within the stated model assumptions.

Shared flow relations

For a calorically perfect gas, the principal relations are

a = sqrt(γRT)                 M = V/a
Tt/T = 1 + (γ − 1)M²/2       pt/p = (Tt/T)^(γ/(γ − 1))
ṁ = ρVA
J = ṁV + pA

J is the stream momentum function: momentum flux plus the absolute-pressure force on a section. It is not thrust. For a nozzle exhausting to ambient pressure pa, gross thrust is Fgross = ṁVe + (pe − pa)Ae. A net airbreathing result also subtracts captured-air flight momentum where the selected full-engine model defines captured flow. A nozzle or flow solution is rejected or stopped when its selected branch, sonic condition, property range, or conservation residual cannot remain valid.

Turbojet and turbofan cycles

Design-point cycle models connect inlet, compressor or fan, combustor, turbine, and nozzle station states. Constant-cp modes use component pressure ratios, efficiencies, prescribed losses, and shaft-work balance. Typical component relations include compressor work wc = cp(Tt,out − Tt,in), turbine work wt = cp(Tt,in − Tt,out), and fuel addition from the selected combustor energy balance.

Turbojet modes include ideal and loss-based design points, afterburning, a fixed-area double-choked off-design reference model, dual-spool afterburning off-design, and standalone inlet, compressor, burner, turbine, nozzle, and compressor-map calculations. Map interpolation is shape-preserving and never extrapolates.

Turbofan modes include separate-flow single-spool design, reference-normalized dual-spool separate-flow off-design, constant-cp dual-spool mixed flow with optional afterburning, standalone components, and NASA9 variable-property design modes. Separate-flow results normalize specific thrust by total inlet air and fuel-air ratio by core air. Mixed-flow models explicitly conserve mass, total enthalpy, and momentum; a constant-area mixer uses the subsonic conservation root and rejects incompatible static-pressure or root conditions.

Ideal-cycle models use a single gas, neglect fuel mass addition, and assume fully expanded streams. They are not component-map or fixed-geometry off-design models unless that is the selected mode. Variable-property modes use dry air at 79 mol% N2 and 21 mol% O2, gaseous H2/CH4/CO fuel, adiabatic gas-phase equilibrium combustion, and frozen downstream composition. They use variable-area fully expanded exits, exclude liquid vaporization, cooling, and condensation, and do not claim map or fixed-geometry matching. Optional core-stream afterburning is downstream of the turbine and does not alter shaft load.

Liquid and solid rockets

Liquid-rocket performance uses chamber pressure pc, throat area At, mass flow ṁ, characteristic velocity, thrust coefficient, and specific impulse:

c* = pc At / ṁ
CF = F / (pc At)
Isp = F / (ṁg0)

Explicit-gas modes provide thrust-chamber and nozzle performance, altitude, area-ratio, and chamber-pressure sweeps, chamber sizing, finite contraction, and reference contours. Sweeps hold the reference scale and explicit gas model fixed. Empirical efficiency affects c* or CF, not the reference ideal exit velocity. Exit-plane pressure is not a wall-pressure, separation, or side-load prediction. Conical and bell outputs are reference geometry; bell geometry is an arc–parabola construction with explicit endpoint angles.

Thermochemical rocket modes use NASA9 gas properties. They can close chamber temperature from supplied gaseous inlet enthalpy and equilibrium, or use a specified chamber condition; downstream expansion can be frozen, along-path equilibrium, or frozen at a chosen post-throat area ratio. Equilibrium solutions retain elemental inventory and determine critical mass flow from the internal maximum of ρV. frozen_mach uses local frozen sound speed. These are ideal-gas gas-phase models without liquid vaporization, condensed phases, wall-pressure prediction, post-separation performance, or side loads.

Gaseous mixture-ratio sweeps use H2/CH4/CO and O2. MR is oxidizer mass divided by fuel mass. Each point uses the same selected chemical model, geometry, and back pressure. The reported best specific impulse is only the largest successful discrete sample.

Solid-rocket equilibrium pressure balances a supplied burn-rate law with choked mass discharge. Transient models integrate end-burning or internal-port regression and chamber mass conservation. They begin from an established choked state; they exclude ignition, erosion, and tail-off. Demonstration parameters are not a propellant model.

Ramjet, scramjet, and one-dimensional flow

Ramjet models include a design-point cycle, a full subsonic-combustion flowpath, normal and oblique shocks, Fanno flow, Rayleigh flow, and quasi-one-dimensional flow. Oblique shock mode requires an explicit weak or strong branch and stops beyond the attached-shock limit. Fanno uses Darcy fD L/D; Rayleigh uses a supplied total-temperature ratio. The full flowpath uses specified inlet recovery, a source-term combustor, and a nozzle whose exit area is calculated from specified exit pressure. It is not fixed-geometry back-pressure matching.

Fixed-cp quasi-one-dimensional models integrate mass, momentum, and total-energy effects of area change, Darcy friction, heat input, and mass injection. NASA9 frozen-flow mode updates properties and sound speed at every step, requires injection with the same composition as the main stream, and includes formation enthalpy in injection total enthalpy. Both retain valid history when a sonic, residual, or property-range event occurs.

Scramjet fixed-cp full-engine, combustor, and nozzle models use the same source-term flow framework. They do not provide reaction kinetics, shock trains, cooling coupling, or automatic mode transition. The seven-state NASA9 reconstruction accepts T/P/M, T/P/V, T/ρ/M, T/ρ/V, P/ρ/M, P/ρ/V, or F/G/ṁ, where

F = pA + ṁV
G = h + V²/2

Here F is the model’s momentum-function input, with the same convention as J = pA + ṁV above; it is not a thrust input. F/G requires an explicit subsonic or supersonic root. In the fixed-cp form, G = cpT + V²/2; it must not receive formation-enthalpy data.

The calibrated variable-property inlet uses NASA9 dry air and either altitude plus Mach, Mach plus dynamic pressure, or altitude plus dynamic pressure. It supports contraction ratio, exit Mach, exit pressure, exit temperature, or total-pressure-recovery closure, and an explicit high- or low-Mach contraction root. Its atmosphere range is 0–70 km and it is an independent inlet model, not a complete reacting-flow match.

Calibrated full-engine models join a dry-air inlet, combustor, and nozzle with supplied geometry. Nonreacting mixing and finite-rate variants are available. Net thrust subtracts captured-air flight momentum; specific_impulse_injected uses all injected mass. The models do not automatically match ambient back pressure, shock trains, or operating-mode transition, and they report no full-engine thrust when a segment is incomplete.

Thermodynamic properties, equilibrium, and finite-rate chemistry

NASA9 frozen-property models use archived NASA/TP-2002-211556 species data in the common 200–6000 K range of selected species. Mixture enthalpy includes formation enthalpy; cp, cv, γ, and entropy follow the selected frozen composition. Property calculation is not chemical equilibrium.

Gas-equilibrium modes solve ideal-gas equilibrium subject to elemental conservation. The current model contains 13 gas species and excludes ions and condensed phases. Constant-pressure energy closure solves for equilibrium temperature from inlet gaseous composition, inlet temperature, pressure, and heat per unit mixture mass; zero heat is the adiabatic case. A user-provided temperature interval must bracket the root.

Finite-rate reactor mode is a constant-volume, externally isothermal mass-action system. It integrates reactions of the form

dcᵢ/dt = Σνᵢⱼ(kf Πcr^νr − kr Πcp^νp)
kf = A T^n exp(−Ea/RuT)

User-supplied reactions and Arrhenius data define the model. The default dissociation data demonstrate the algorithm only. The optional hydrogen–oxygen submechanism has 10 species and 29 reactions, including third-body efficiencies and Lindemann/Troe falloff. It uses NASA7 data over the common 300–3500 K range. It is not a full GRI-Mech or a reproduction of another solver. Finite-rate reacting-flow mode couples species mass flow, momentum, and total energy in a quasi-one-dimensional segment; it omits transport, diffusion, and automatic transonic matching.

TBCC, RBCC, and ATR submodels

TBCC combines independent turbojet and ramjet results using an explicit captured-air split. It does not solve common-inlet matching, variable geometry, back-pressure interaction, or transition control.

RBCC is an ideal constant-area ejector-ramjet submodel with specified primary total state, supersonic primary flow, subsonic secondary flow, a choked ejector exit, and fully expanded output. It is not a full RBCC multimode cycle.

ATR is an ideal air-turboramjet submodel with specified primary total state, ideal turbine–fan work balance, low-speed mixing, simplified heat addition, and fully expanded output. It excludes gas-generator, pump, and cooling models.

Interpretation

No model should be treated as an engine certification, a full operating envelope, or a substitute for experimental validation. Demonstration cases are workflow examples. The in-app mode description identifies the active calculation and its limits; exported results preserve that model mode and the SI input state used to obtain them.
