ThrustOne User Guide

ThrustOne supports Apple silicon Macs running macOS 12 or later, and iPads running iPadOS 16 or later.

This guide is generated from the capability catalog, field catalog, model descriptions, and demonstration cases. Demonstration results help identify controls and workflows; they do not represent a specific engine design, performance certification, or full-envelope prediction.

ThrustOne Engine Performance Calculator

Getting started Choose an engine and analysis task, adjust the conditions, then select Calculate (Command-Return). Return or leaving a field commits its value; Escape restores it. Decimal numbers, scientific notation, and the Unicode minus sign are accepted. Units appear beside fields. Internal calculations and exports use SI values. A curve and its results always belong to one complete input state. A changed input makes earlier results stale; recalculate before exporting.

Requirements ThrustOne supports Apple silicon Macs running macOS 12 or later, and iPads running iPadOS 16 or later.

Available calculations • Turbojet: ideal and loss-based design points, afterburning, explicit-reference double-choked and dual-spool afterburning off-design points, and five standalone components. Compressor map files use shape-preserving interpolation and are never extrapolated. • Turbofan: separate-flow single-spool design, reference-normalized dual-spool separate-flow off-design, and constant-cp dual-spool mixed-flow or mixed-afterburning analyses. Constant-cp mixing matches inlet static pressure and uses the subsonic conservation root. NASA9 variable-cp design analyses include separate flow, mixed flow, and independent post-mixer afterburning. Specific thrust uses total inlet air; fuel-air ratio uses core air. Ideal modes neglect fuel mass addition and assume fully expanded streams. • Liquid rocket: thrust-chamber and nozzle performance with explicit gas properties; altitude, area-ratio, and chamber-pressure sweeps at fixed reference scale; equivalent cylindrical sizing; cylinder-plus-convergent volume allocation; a standalone finite-contraction static chamber plane; and conical or bell reference geometry. Mixture-ratio sweeps never combine values based on different chemical assumptions. • Ramjet: full-engine design point, normal shock, explicit weak or strong oblique shock, Darcy Fanno friction, same-branch Rayleigh heating, and fixed-cp quasi-one-dimensional flow. Flow segments support area, Darcy friction, heat flow, and injected mass. They stop at sonic events or residual limits and retain valid history. • Solid rocket: equilibrium pressure from a supplied burn-rate law and end-burning or internal-port regression transients. Demonstration parameters do not represent a specific propellant. Models start from an established choked state and exclude ignition, erosion, and tail-off blowdown. • TBCC: independent turbojet and ramjet channels combined with an explicit air split. The shared freestream is defined in turbojet fields. Common-inlet matching, variable geometry, and automatic transition are not solved. • RBCC: an ideal ejector-ramjet submodel with specified primary total state, supersonic primary flow, subsonic secondary flow, and a choked ejector exit. It is not a full multimode RBCC cycle. • ATR: an ideal air-turboramjet submodel with specified primary total state, ideal work balance, low-speed mixing, and simplified heating. It excludes a gas generator, pump, and cooling.

Freestream and model limits Turbojet input supports Mach plus ambient state, altitude plus Mach, Mach plus dynamic pressure, and altitude plus dynamic pressure. Altitude is geometric; the standard atmosphere is valid from 0 to 86 km. Legacy JSON altitude values in km convert to m on import. The dynamic-pressure path uses standard air with γ = 1.4.

Ideal cycles use one gas and neglect fuel mass addition. Other constant-cp cycles use supplied cold- and hot-side heat capacities and component losses. Constant-cp calculation is not chemical equilibrium. Liquid-rocket empirical efficiencies correct characteristic velocity or thrust coefficient; they do not change the ideal reference exit velocity. Exit-plane static pressure is not a validated wall-pressure or side-load prediction.

Cases and files Open and save native JSON cases up to 10 MiB and 64 nesting levels. Version 2 uses engine, mode, and the associated input section. Unsupported modes report an error; they are never silently replaced. Some legacy turbojet cases and rocket cases with explicit reference conditions can import; other thermodynamic-table branches have not been migrated.

Edits in JSON Interchange update the form only after Apply JSON. Restore Form Contents discards unapplied text, which is not written to the saved case. Unknown fields are retained as extension data; known fields in this version determine the calculation. The File menu provides Save, Save As, Close, and multiple windows.

Studies and exports Parameter Study operates on currently exposed independent fields. It accepts linear endpoints or explicit value lists, one- or two-dimensional grids, and at most 1,000 total cases. It shows progress and can be cancelled while retaining completed work. Failed rows retain their error. Liquid-rocket plots do not join across failed points. History tables show the first 100 points; JSON, CSV, and graphic exports retain all data.

Study axes, explicit lists, and the two-dimensional switch are saved with a case. The comparison metric and maximum/minimum choice are view state. Grid extrema cover only successful computed points; they are neither continuous nor global optima. A three-point curve may show an endpoint secant slope; reduce the step to check local stability.

Metric Constraints and Comparison Report apply inclusive SI lower and upper bounds to existing results; every selected constraint must pass. Apply the constraints first. Reapplying the same metric replaces its bound; Remove removes it. Rows with missing metrics or errors cannot qualify. The report records the request, axes, constraints, extrema before and after filtering, and each row’s status. Filtering does not rerun the solver and is not continuous optimization or a complete performance envelope.

One-dimensional studies provide local golden-section search. Inspect objective shape with a grid first, specify an interval, and declare that it can be treated as unimodal there. The search uses 4–100 evaluations and stops on combined absolute and relative interval-width tolerances. An out-of-domain, non-finite, missing-metric, or constraint-violating point stops it; that point is not skipped. The result is only the best evaluated point. Budget exhaustion is not convergence, and interval convergence neither proves a global optimum nor bounds objective error. Settings are view state; full conditions and evaluated points export as a separate JSON record.

A history plot can overlay one same-unit series: blue circles are the primary quantity and orange squares the overlay. The legend and numeric table show both. Each series retains its gaps; failed points are not joined. Different dimensions never share a forced vertical axis. A two-dimensional grid shows a discrete feasible set: passing points are colored by objective, red crosses retain solver failures, and gray diamonds retain rejected or missing-metric points. It does not interpolate or draw a continuous envelope. PNG and PDF export are available; the comparison report contains all raw rows and reasons.

Result JSON contains the case used, model mode, SI values, and histories. CSV uses UTF-8 with BOM and CRLF, with every quantity’s unit and an input snapshot. PNG is 1000 × 600; PDF is vector graphics. Stale results cannot be exported as current results.

Offline and privacy Calculations run locally. ThrustOne does not connect to remote services, upload cases, or use model APIs. Files are saved only where you choose.

Scope ThrustOne provides 49 runnable calculation models across turbojets, turbofans, liquid and solid rockets, ramjets, scramjets, TBCC, RBCC, and ATR. The capability catalog and current mode description state each model’s conditions and limits. Interpret results only within the selected model. Demonstration inputs explain operation; they do not represent a specific engine design, performance certification, or full-envelope prediction.

The form footer can switch display between SI and engineering units (kPa, kN, kJ); primary results convert and the preference is saved with the case. Commit any field being edited before switching. The calculation core, JSON, CSV, station data, curves, and explicit lists always use the labeled SI values. Temperature remains K.

Thermochemistry and variable-property models Scramjet full-engine, combustor, and fixed-cp frozen-nozzle modes use explicit heat and mass sources and stop at sonic events. In seven-state fixed-cp F/G input, F = pA + ṁV in N and G = cpT + V²/2 in J/kg. Do not enter reacting-flow total enthalpy containing species formation enthalpies in that form.

NASA9 properties use archived U.S. government species data (NASA/TP-2002-211556) only in the common 200–6000 K range of selected species. Enthalpy includes formation enthalpy; pressure is Pa and molar mass is kg/kmol. Gas equilibrium uses 13 species with no ions or condensed phases. Displayed cp and γ are local frozen-composition properties.

Variable-cp frozen nozzles solve enthalpy, entropy, and mass continuity. Thermochemical rocket tasks can solve chamber equilibrium at specified chamber temperature and pressure, then expand with frozen composition. Chamber temperature can also close from gaseous inlet enthalpy and equilibrium combustion. Along-path equilibrium and user-selected downstream-area freezing are available. These models exclude liquid-propellant vaporization and condensed phases.

Finite-rate reactor mode is constant-volume and externally isothermal. You supply reactions and Arrhenius coefficients. A uses kmol/m³ concentrations and K; Ea uses J/kmol. Default coefficients demonstrate the algorithm and are not a combustion mechanism. Reverse rates derive from selected thermodynamic data. The supplied hydrogen–oxygen 10-species/29-reaction option includes third-body and Lindemann/Troe corrections. Stiffness or step limits retain an incomplete state, which cannot participate in study extrema.

The ramjet path’s final nozzle uses specified exit pressure and back-calculates area. It shows two stations and is not fixed-geometry back-pressure matching. A bell nozzle is arc–parabola geometry with explicit endpoint angles; it is not a validated wall-pressure, separation, or side-load prediction.

Constant-pressure energy closure uses gaseous inlet composition, common inlet temperature, and heat per unit mixture mass to find equilibrium temperature. Zero heat is adiabatic. Every temperature trial must satisfy elemental conservation and equilibrium residuals; an unbracketed user temperature interval is rejected. There is no liquid vaporization, condensed phase, or ion model.

Thermochemical rocket analysis permits specified chamber temperature or inlet-energy closure. Gaseous mixture-ratio sweeps use H2/CH4/CO with O2; MR is oxidizer-to-fuel mass ratio. Every point keeps geometry and back pressure fixed and uses the same chamber-equilibrium and selected nozzle-chemistry model. Best Isp means the maximum among successful samples; failed rows remain and no continuous optimum is inferred.

Variable-cp turbojet and separate-flow turbofan components use NASA9 frozen composition and h(T), s(T,p) inversion. Inlet uses supplied total-pressure recovery only. Compressor and turbine use separately entered isentropic efficiencies; do not treat a previous polytropic efficiency as the same parameter. Nozzles expand to specified exit static pressure and do not perform fixed-geometry matching. This path has no compressor map or combustion reaction. Fan, booster, and bypass nozzle share these components; the mixer has its selected mixing model.

NASA9 turbojet and separate-flow turbofan design points use dry air at 79 mol% N2 and 21 mol% O2. Gaseous H2/CH4/CO fuel is specified by fuel-to-core-air mass ratio, and fuel inlet temperature is included separately in formation enthalpy. The combustor solves adiabatic gas-phase equilibrium, then freezes composition. The turbine drives core-compressor and all-air fan loads. Net specific thrust uses total air; fuel-air ratio uses core air. These modes use fully expanded variable-area exits, not fixed geometry, maps, or off-design matching. They exclude liquid vaporization, cooling, and condensation.

The calibrated variable-property inlet supports altitude plus Mach, Mach plus dynamic pressure, and altitude plus dynamic pressure with contraction ratio, exit Mach, exit static pressure, exit static temperature, or total-pressure recovery. It uses NASA9 dry air and 0–70 km geometric altitude. A contraction inversion explicitly chooses the higher- or lower-Mach root. It does not extrapolate higher cold-atmosphere properties or promise point-for-point agreement with another mechanism database.

NASA9 frozen quasi-one-dimensional flow updates heat capacity and sound speed at every step and checks total-energy residual. Injection must have main-stream composition; injection total enthalpy includes formation enthalpy and may be physically negative. After changing composition, select Update Mass Flow from Inlet State. Sonic, property-range, and residual events retain a valid prefix, which cannot be used for study extrema. It does not mix different compositions or solve finite-rate chemistry.

NASA9 seven-state input supports T/P/M, T/P/V, T/ρ/M, T/ρ/V, P/ρ/M, P/ρ/V, and F/G/ṁ. Here F = pA + ṁV and G = h + V²/2; G includes formation enthalpy. F/G requires an explicit subsonic or supersonic root.

Finite-rate reacting flow integrates species mass flow, momentum, and total energy, so temperature changes with reaction. Each segment may specify injection composition; absent a value, inlet composition is used. Injection total enthalpy includes formation enthalpy, so external heat must not add fuel reaction heat again. Default dissociation coefficients demonstrate the algorithm only. Supply reliable mechanism parameters for a physical study. The model omits transport, diffusion, and automatic transonic matching.

The calibrated full engine connects a dry-air inlet, combustor, and nozzle in either nonreacting mixing or finite-rate mode. Net thrust subtracts captured-air flight momentum. specific_impulse_injected uses all injected mass; air or oxidizer is not automatically called fuel. Geometry is supplied by the user; ambient back-pressure closure, shock trains, and mode transition are not automatic. If any segment is incomplete, full-engine thrust is not reported.

Equilibrium nozzles follow an isentropic path with the same elemental inventory; critical mass flow is found from the internal maximum of ρV. Selected-area freezing switches to fixed composition at the user’s post-throat area ratio. The local frozen Mach there must exceed 1; equilibrium throat state is not automatically a frozen-sonic throat state. The selected point is not a calculated chemical-freeze location. frozen_mach uses local frozen sound speed, not equilibrium sound speed. Property extrapolation is rejected. Current models contain ideal gas only: no liquid vaporization, condensation, wall pressure, post-separation performance, or side load.

The optional hydrogen–oxygen submechanism has 10 species and 29 reactions, including HO2/H2O2, third-body efficiencies, and Troe reactions. Source cm/mol/s and cal/mol values are converted to m/kmol/s and J/kmol. It is neither a full GRI-Mech nor a point-for-point reproduction of other software. NASA9 and hydrogen–oxygen NASA7 are independent selections. The H2/O2 mechanism common range is 300–3500 K. A standalone flow stops outside it or at the integration budget; calibrated full-engine analysis uses NASA9.

The reaction editor can create elementary, third-body, or pressure-falloff reactions. Unlisted collision efficiencies equal 1. Pressure falloff uses Lindemann with optional three- or four-parameter Troe. Units of A vary with reaction order and temperature exponent; check displayed units after changing type. Load replaces reactions, initial composition, and reactor controls. The included data are adapted from the Cantera hydrogen–oxygen submechanism; its BSD license appears under Third-Party Data Licenses. Presets only enter the workflow and do not replace experimental or textbook validation for a specific engine.

Mixture-ratio sweeps can use chamber-exit freezing, along-path equilibrium, or selected-area freezing. Every point uses the same selected model, geometry, and back pressure; older files default to chamber-exit freezing. Failed samples retain their reason. The highest discrete specific impulse is not a continuous optimum.

Variable-cp turbojet and separate-flow turbofan models offer optional core-stream afterburning. Afterburner fuel-air ratio is per core-air mass and uses the same gaseous fuel as the main combustor. Fuel inlet temperature enters formation enthalpy separately; lower heating value is not added again. The afterburner follows the turbine and does not change shaft loads; separate-flow bypass does not afterburn. Total fuel flow and specific fuel consumption include main combustor and afterburner. This mode excludes mixed flow, liquid vaporization, cooling, and map matching.

The turbofan two-stream mixer can use NASA9 variable-property frozen mixing. Enter both streams’ total states, mass flows, compositions, and core inlet Mach. Bypass static pressure matches the core, and areas are recovered from both mass flows; a constant-area exit uses only the subsonic conservation root. Composition mixes by elemental and species mass flow without chemical reaction. Total-pressure recovery is a separate loss downstream of the mixer and does not alter its momentum conservation. This component is not a mixed-flow full-engine match or FAIR/VSH validation.

Offline theory manual Choose Open Theory Manual in Help to read the bundled English reference. Search for a term, move between matches, follow the PDF contents links, or enter a page number. No internet connection is required. Each chapter states its assumptions, equations and interpretation limits.
